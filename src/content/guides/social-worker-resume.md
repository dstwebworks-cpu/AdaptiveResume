A social worker resume should answer three questions early. What license do you hold, and at what level? Which practice area do you know? And how many people or families did you really carry, and what did you do for them? A resume that says "provided case management and support services to vulnerable populations" could describe nearly anyone in human services.

Your license needs special care on the page. Each state names its social work licenses in its own way and sets its own rules about who can practice independently. This guide shows you how to write your license, your practice area, and your caseload accurately, in words a hiring manager and screening software can both find.

## The caseload line: your practice in one sentence

Put one line under each job that tells a hiring manager the people you served and how many.

**Before:** "Social worker responsible for providing case management and support services to clients and families."

**After:** "Social worker, [Licensed Clinical Social Worker (LCSW)] ([state], active); [300]-bed hospital, [oncology and medical] units; [25] active patients; psychosocial assessments, discharge planning, and [community and hospice] referrals; [Epic] documentation."

Confidentiality comes first in this work. Keep names and identifying stories off the page. Write your numbers from memory and from records you own, and do not go back into a former employer's case system to collect them. If you signed a severance or confidentiality agreement, read it before you write any number, because it is a contract and breaking it can put your severance at risk. The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission [explains what it means to give up legal claims in a severance agreement](https://www.eeoc.gov/laws/guidance/qa-understanding-waivers-discrimination-claims-employee-severance-agreements), and an employment attorney can tell you what yours allows.

## Your license level, in your state's words

State boards license social workers at different levels, and the names differ. You may see a bachelor's-level license such as LBSW (Licensed Baccalaureate Social Worker), a master's-level license such as LMSW (Licensed Master Social Worker), or a clinical license such as LCSW (Licensed Clinical Social Worker) or LICSW (Licensed Independent Clinical Social Worker), depending on the state. Two people with the same training can hold licenses with different names, so write exactly what your board issued: "[License name], [state] #[N], active."

**The exam.** Many state boards use exams from ASWB (the Association of Social Work Boards), which come in four categories: Bachelors, Masters, Advanced Generalist, and Clinical. You do not need to list the exam once you are licensed. If you are not licensed yet, you can say when you plan to take it.

**Supervised hours.** Clinical licenses require supervised work after your degree, and each state sets the number and the rules. If you are partway there, state your hours and your supervisor's license type, and leave out the supervisor's name.

**Your degree.** List your BSW (Bachelor of Social Work) or MSW (Master of Social Work) with the school and year. Social work programs are accredited by CSWE (the Council on Social Work Education), and some postings ask for an accredited degree.

**The compact.** The Social Work Licensure Compact has been enacted in many states, but list a multistate license only once you actually hold one.

Many states limit who may use the title "social worker," so check your board's rules. If you do not meet your state's rule for the title, describe your job by what it was, such as case manager or family support specialist.

## Credentials, exactly as held

Our guide on [how to list certifications on a resume](/guides/how-to-list-certifications-on-resume/) covers where they go. Beyond your license, list only what you hold.

- **CCM.** The Board-Certified Case Manager (CCM) credential comes from CCMC (the Commission for Case Manager Certification). Include it if you earned it.
- **NASW credentials.** If you hold a credential from NASW (the National Association of Social Workers), write it exactly as issued, with its current status.
- **Substance use credentials.** These are usually issued by a state certification board and go by different names. Write yours exactly as your state issued it.
- **Treatment training.** Be precise about what you completed. "Completed [N]-hour DBT (dialectical behavior therapy) skills training" is accurate if a training is all you finished. "EMDR (eye movement desensitization and reprocessing) certified" should be written only if you hold the certification from EMDRIA (the EMDR International Association).

## Say which practice area you know

**Hospitals and health care.** Show your units, your caseload, psychosocial assessments, discharge planning, advance directives, and the community resources you connected people to. Show how you worked with nurses, physicians, and case managers.

**Child welfare.** Show the number of families or children you carried, investigations or ongoing cases, home visits per month, court reports you wrote, and your work as a mandated reporter. Name your state's child welfare case management system only if your agreements with your employer allow it.

**Before:** "Provided case management to children and families in the child welfare system."

**After:** "Child welfare case worker, [county] agency; [18] ongoing [foster care and in-home] family cases; [36] home visits per month; wrote court reports for [quarterly] reviews."

**Schools.** Show your student caseload, grade levels, crisis response, attendance work, and your part in IEPs (individualized education programs). Student records are protected, so write in numbers.

**Mental health.** Show your client load, the sessions you held per week, and the treatment approaches you actually use, such as CBT (cognitive behavioral therapy), DBT, or motivational interviewing. If your license allows you to diagnose, say you used the DSM-5-TR (the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision). Tools can include the PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9), the GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale), and the C-SSRS (Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale).

**Substance use.** Show the level of care, group and individual counts, and your use of the ASAM Criteria (American Society of Addiction Medicine).

**Hospice and aging services.** Show your caseload, your work with the care team, family meetings, grief support, and your knowledge of Medicare and Medicaid benefits.

**Community agencies.** Show program size, grant reporting, outreach numbers, and the benefit programs you helped people apply for.

## Your assessments, your crisis work, and your systems

**Assessment.** Say which assessments you wrote, how many each week, and whether you did them independently.

**Crisis work.** Say what you were trained to do and how often you did it, including safety planning and involuntary hold procedures where you were authorized to take part.

**Supervision.** If you supervised staff, students, or licensure candidates, say how many and in what capacity.

**Program and policy work.** Include programs you built, grants you helped manage, and audits or accreditation visits you supported, such as those by The Joint Commission or CARF (Commission on Accreditation of Rehabilitation Facilities).

**Systems.** Name Epic, Oracle Health (formerly Cerner), TherapyNotes, SimplePractice, or your agency's platform. Screening software looks for product names.

## Format notes

**How long it should be depends on your work history.** One page is right for a new graduate. Two pages is normal once you have more than one substantive role to describe. Put your license near the top in its own labeled section, and start each job with your caseload line.

Federal applications through USAJOBS generally cap at two pages as of the September 2025 OPM (Office of Personnel Management) change. The VA (Department of Veterans Affairs) says that limit does not apply to its social worker jobs, so follow the resume instructions in the job announcement.

Start with your latest job and keep everything in one column, with standard headings and real text. Screening software reads plain layouts most reliably, so skip graphics and tables.

## Social work bullets you can adapt, keeping only what's true

- "[License name], [state], active; [setting]; caseload of [N] [patients / families / students]"
- "Completed [N] psychosocial assessments per week and coordinated discharge plans with [N]-member care teams"
- "Managed [N] ongoing child welfare cases; completed [N] home visits per month and wrote [N] court reports per quarter"
- "Provided [CBT / DBT / motivational interviewing] to [N] clients per week; screened with [PHQ-9 / GAD-7 / C-SSRS]"
- "Connected [N] clients per month to [housing / Medicaid / food assistance / hospice] resources"
- "Led [N] weekly groups of [N] participants at [level of care]"
- "Supervised [N] [BSW / MSW] interns and [N] licensure candidates"
- "Completed [N] of [N] supervised clinical hours toward [license name] in [state]"

Before you keep a bullet, make sure you could explain it to a hiring manager.

## What screening software looks for on a social worker resume

Terms that often appear in social work postings include social worker, LMSW, LCSW, MSW, BSW, psychosocial assessment, case management, discharge planning, crisis intervention, safety planning, treatment plan, mandated reporter, trauma-informed care, motivational interviewing, HIPAA (the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act), and your settings and systems by name. Use a term only if you have done the work behind it.

Federal job data splits this work by area. [The U.S. Department of Labor's Occupational Information Network, or O*NET, lists Healthcare Social Workers under 21-1022.00](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/21-1022.00), and it has separate entries for child, family, and school social workers and for mental health and substance use social workers. Make your own practice area clear near the top of your page.

Be most careful with your license level and what it allows. "LCSW" before the license is issued, a diagnosis your license does not allow, or "certified" for a course you attended can each be checked quickly.
