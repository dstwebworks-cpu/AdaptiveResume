Project management resumes have a specific disease: they're written in the language of project management instead of the language of projects. "Drove cross-functional stakeholder alignment across the delivery lifecycle" says nothing a reader can weigh. What gets a PM resume through the pile is scope — the size of what you ran and what happened because you ran it.

## The four numbers every PM bullet wants

For each project or role, try to answer honestly:

- **Budget** — what dollar amount did you own or track?
- **People** — how many, across how many teams or vendors?
- **Timeline** — how long, and against what deadline pressure?
- **Outcome** — delivered when? Under budget by what? What did it fix?

**Before:** "Managed software implementation projects using Agile methodology."
**After:** "Ran a 9-month ERP rollout across 3 departments (~$400k budget, 12-person mixed team of staff and vendor); delivered two weeks early after re-scoping the data-migration phase."

Estimates marked honestly ("~", "about") are fine. A number you can't defend in the interview is not — everything on the page should survive "how did you get that figure?"

## Certifications: list what you hold, exactly

PMP, CAPM, CSM, PMI-ACP, PRINCE2 — these are screening keywords, so name them precisely, with the year if current. Two honesty rules: an expired credential is listed as expired or left off, and "studying for PMP" is not a certification — if you want it on the page, the honest form is a "currently pursuing" line, clearly separated from credentials you hold.

## Methodology keywords without the stuffing

ATS filters do look for Agile, Scrum, Kanban, waterfall — but a bare keyword list reads as padding to the human who opens the file next. Attach each methodology to a project where you actually used it: "ran two-week sprints with a 6-person dev team" earns the keyword "Scrum" the honest way.

## No PM title yet? Claim the scope, not the rank

Many of the strongest PM candidates were never called PM — coordinators, leads, office managers, sergeants who ran the moving parts of something real. Don't invent a title you didn't hold. Claim the project itself: what you were given, what you coordinated, what got delivered. "Coordinated the warehouse relocation — 3 vendors, 6-week window, zero missed ship days" is project management evidence under any title. (Same principle as our [promotions guide](/guides/how-to-list-promotions-on-resume): scope is the honest claim; titles are just labels.)

## Format notes for PM resumes

One page if under ~10 years, two if more. A compact skills block near the top (tools: Jira, MS Project, Smartsheet; methods; certifications) for the keyword scan — then bullets that prove each item in the block. Everything in the block must reappear in a project; a skills list with no evidence attached is where PM resumes lose trust.
