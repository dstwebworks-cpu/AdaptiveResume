Claims managers read an adjuster resume for scope before anything else: what kind of claims you handle, whether you work them from a desk or in the field, how much you can settle on your own, and how many files you carry. A resume that says "handled claims from first notice of loss to settlement" describes every adjuster at once, so it tells a hiring manager nothing about you.

The fix is a scope line at the top of each job, then the numbers your claims work is measured by, then your licenses and software named the way a recruiter searches for them.

## Lead with your scope and your numbers

**Before:** "Claims Adjuster handling property claims from first notice of loss to settlement."

**After:** "Property Field Adjuster (staff), homeowners claims: carried 110 to 130 open files, settled within $25,000 authority, held a 21-day average cycle time with estimates in Xactimate, and worked 2 catastrophe deployments after hail and wind events."

The second version answers a claims manager's first four questions in one line: the claim type, field or desk, how you were employed, and how much you could settle. Then it gives caseload, cycle time, and catastrophe experience, which are the numbers a manager compares between candidates.

## Write the scope line first

Every adjusting job is a mix of a few choices, and the scope line names yours:

- **Claim type:** property, auto physical damage, bodily injury, general liability, workers' compensation, or commercial lines.
- **Desk or field:** inside adjusters work by phone and documents; field adjusters inspect the loss in person.
- **How you were employed:** staff with one insurer, independent through an adjusting firm, with a third-party administrator, or as a public adjuster working for policyholders.
- **Personal or commercial lines,** and the size of the losses you handled if it sets you apart.

"Auto Desk Adjuster, bodily injury, staff" and "Independent Field Adjuster, residential property" are two different jobs, and the scope line lets a recruiter tell them apart without reading further. Your summary can pull it together in one line, for example: "Independent property field adjuster licensed in 4 states, with 3 catastrophe deployments and Xactimate estimates up to $150,000."

## The numbers a claims manager reads

- **Caseload.** Your open claims at a typical point, or the range you carried.
- **Closings.** Claims you closed in a typical month.
- **Settlement authority.** The dollar amount you could settle without a supervisor's approval. It is the quickest way to show how much an employer trusted your judgment.
- **Cycle time.** Your average days from first notice of loss to closing, or to first contact and first inspection if your employer tracked those.
- **Quality.** File audit scores, reinspection results, or compliance reviews, written the way your employer reported them.
- **Recoveries.** Subrogation or salvage recoveries, if you handled them.
- **Catastrophe work.** Each deployment, with the event type, the dates, and the claims you handled.

Use only figures you know from your own work, never reports copied out of an employer's systems, and keep everything about a claim itself private: no claimant names, claim numbers, or details from a file.

## Licenses, named exactly

Adjuster licensing is set by each state. Many states require a license and some do not, and adjusters who travel often hold licenses in several states. If you live in a state that does not license adjusters, another state can issue you a designated home state license. The Texas Department of Insurance, for example, describes its [designated home state all-lines adjuster license](https://agate.tdi.texas.gov/agent/adjuster-designated-home-state-apply.html) as "the special non-resident license," and says only residents of states that don't license adjusters can apply.

Write your licenses in one compact line so a recruiter can scan them: "Adjuster licenses: Texas (designated home state, all lines), Florida, Georgia, Louisiana." Many states also require continuing education to keep a license current, so check that each one is active before you list it. Public adjusters, who work for the policyholder, hold a separate public adjuster license in some states; Texas, for example, licenses public insurance adjusters under a separate chapter of its Insurance Code. Write that license by the name your state uses.

## Designations and training

- **AIC (Associate in Claims),** from The Institutes, is a claims designation built around claims handling, with electives in areas such as auto, liability, property, and workers' compensation. Write it the way your certificate prints it, or "AIC candidate, The Institutes" if you are working toward it.
- **Estimating software training.** Xactimate, from Verisk, is widely used for property estimates. If you have finished a training course or a certification level, name it as your certificate shows it.
- **Carrier and firm training** that ended in a certificate, such as a catastrophe readiness course or a line-specific program.

## Software and systems

Name the estimating tools and claim systems you used every day, because many postings ask for a specific one. Property adjusters usually name Xactimate. Auto adjusters and appraisers often name estimating platforms such as CCC ONE, Mitchell, or Audatex. Claim systems such as Guidewire ClaimCenter are worth naming too. Add the tools you used in the field, such as drone inspections, or the ladder-assist services you scheduled, if they were part of the job.

## Moving into claims from another job

Many adjusters start somewhere else, and the resume should connect that work to claims:

- **Customer service** shows call volume, de-escalation, and documentation. Write the volume: "Handled 60 to 80 customer calls a day with full notes in the account system."
- **Construction, roofing, or restoration** shows you can read damage and write an estimate. Name the trades and the estimate sizes.
- **Auto repair or body work** shows you can judge damage and repair costs on vehicles.
- **Any job with careful records,** such as medical billing or law enforcement reports, shows the documentation habit claims work depends on.

Add the adjuster license you hold or the one you are studying for, and say which claim type you want in your summary.

## Format notes

- **Put the scope line directly under each job title,** before the bullets.
- **Keep licenses and designations in their own section** near the top, because a recruiter for an adjusting firm will look for the states you are licensed in.
- **Group catastrophe deployments** under the firm that sent you, one line per event.
- **Keep the layout plain,** with standard headings and no tables, so screening software reads every line.

## Claims adjuster resume bullets you can adapt — keep only what's true

- "[Claim type] [desk/field] adjuster carrying [number] open claims with $[amount] settlement authority."
- "Closed an average of [number] claims a month with a [number]-day average cycle time."
- "Wrote [number] property estimates in Xactimate, with [result of reinspections or audits]."
- "Deployed to [event type], [month/year], [state]; handled [number] claims as [role]."
- "Recovered $[amount] through subrogation on [number] files."
- "Scored [score] on [quarterly file audits or quality reviews] over [period]."
