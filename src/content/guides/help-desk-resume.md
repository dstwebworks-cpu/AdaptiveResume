Help desk hiring answers a practical question: if we put this person on the queue Monday, do tickets get resolved or bounced? Your resume answers it with specifics — the systems you worked in, the volume you carried, what you fixed yourself versus escalated. "Provided technical support to end users" answers nothing; every applicant says it. Here's how to write the version only you can claim.

## The help desk resume vocabulary that screening filters read

Recruiters and screening software search for the working language of the job. Where these are true of you, they belong on the page in exactly these words:

- **Ticketing systems** — ServiceNow, Zendesk, Jira Service Management, Freshservice: name the one you actually worked in, not the category.
- **Queue metrics** — ticket volume, SLA compliance, first-call resolution: the numbers your team lived by.
- **Tier scope** — tier 1, 2, or 3, and where your escalation line sat.
- **The daily stack** — Active Directory (password resets, account lockouts, group membership), Microsoft 365 administration, VPN and remote support tools, MDM enrollment, device imaging and deployment.

The keyword only earns its place if you can take an interview question on it. This is translation of real experience into searchable terms — never decoration.

## Scope first: the line that frames every role

Give each support role a one-line scope statement before the bullets — user count, environment, and tier:

**Before:** "Provided technical support for company employees and resolved computer issues."

**After:** "Tier 1/2 support for ~800 users across 3 sites; ~35 tickets/day in ServiceNow; escalation line to infrastructure for server and network faults."

Twelve seconds in, a hiring manager knows your load, your tools, and your level. Use your real numbers — approximations marked "~" are honest and normal; ticket counts you see every shift are exactly the kind of figure you can defend.

## Numbers a help desk resume can actually claim

You work in one of the most measured jobs there is — the ticketing system counts everything. Honest sources for figures: your daily or weekly ticket volume, your team's SLA targets and how you tracked against them, first-call-resolution if your queue measured it, users supported, devices imaged or deployed, and knowledge-base articles you wrote. If your shop never showed you a dashboard, estimate from what you know — tickets per shift times shifts per week — and mark it approximate. Never quote a precise percentage you never saw.

## The cert block: exactly as held

Certifications get their own labeled section, because screening filters key on it:

- **CompTIA A+** — the standard entry and tier 1 filter.
- **CompTIA Network+ / Security+** — the step-up signals for tier 2+.
- **ITIL Foundation** — meaningful in shops that run formal incident/problem/change management.
- **Microsoft credentials** — worth listing where they match the employer's stack.

Format: full name, issuer, date or current-through. In progress? "A+ — exam scheduled [month/year]" is honest and worth a line; a bare "A+" you haven't earned is a fabrication that credential verification catches.

## A help desk resume bullet bank — keep only what's true

Adapt these, fill in your real numbers, and delete anything that isn't yours:

- "Resolved ~[N] tickets/day in [ServiceNow/Zendesk/Jira Service Management], meeting [SLA target] response and resolution targets"
- "Tier [1/2] support for [N] users across [N] sites/departments"
- "Handled password resets, account lockouts, and group-membership changes in Active Directory"
- "Administered Microsoft 365 accounts: provisioning, license assignment, mailbox troubleshooting"
- "Imaged and deployed [N] laptops/desktops per [week/month] using [tool]"
- "Enrolled and managed mobile devices in [Intune/Jamf/other MDM]"
- "Improved first-call resolution on my queue by [honest change] after [what you actually did]"
- "Wrote [N] knowledge-base articles adopted by the team, cutting repeat questions on [topic]"
- "Escalated server, network, and application faults to tier 3 with full triage notes"
- "Trained [N] new technicians on ticket handling and escalation procedures"

Every bracket is a prompt for your real figure — not permission to invent one.

## What this occupation is called elsewhere

Job titles for this work vary a lot — help desk analyst, desktop support technician, IT support specialist, technical support specialist all describe the same core occupation, which the Department of Labor tracks as [Computer User Support Specialists (O*NET 15-1232.00)](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-1232.00). O*NET's profile lists those titles among the ones actually reported by people in the role, and names help desk software, ServiceNow, and Active Directory in its technology skills — useful confirmation that this vocabulary is the occupation's real language, not jargon we made up. Search postings under all of those titles; your resume's skills section should read naturally against any of them.

## Format notes

One page for under ~8 years of experience. Single column, standard headings, certifications in their own block, scope line atop each role. No skill-rating bars — "Active Directory" as selectable text is searchable; a four-star graphic is invisible to parsing software.
