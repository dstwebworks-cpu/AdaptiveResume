A bus driver resume is read in a particular order. The endorsement comes first: a transit agency needs the P endorsement, a school district needs the S endorsement, and no amount of experience substitutes for either. Then the record, because a driver responsible for forty passengers or sixty children is hired on safety before anything else. Then the routes, the passengers, and the equipment.

Most bus driver resumes get this order backward. They open with "responsible for transporting passengers safely" and leave the reader to hunt for the license, the endorsement, and the numbers. Put the license line at the top, written exactly, and put real numbers on the work.

## Lead with the license line, written exactly

**Before:** "CDL driver with passenger endorsement, experienced with school buses and transit."

**After:** "CDL Class B, Georgia, expires [month/year]. Endorsements: P (passenger), S (school bus). Air brake restriction: none. DOT medical certificate current through [month/year]. Georgia school bus driver certification [year]; CPR and first aid current through [month/year]."

![Before and after example of an improved resume line - every line confirmed by the person](/img/guides/examples/bus-driver-resume-example.png)

The second version answers the screening questions in one block and uses the words the posting uses. Write only what the license shows. The S endorsement requires its own knowledge and skills tests, and since February 2022, a first-time P or S endorsement also requires Entry-Level Driver Training through a registered provider. If you completed it, list the provider and the date. If you were endorsed before then, it does not apply, and you should not claim it.

## The parts of the job that carry the most weight

**Routes and passengers, in numbers.** Daily routes, stops, passengers per day, and route type: fixed-route transit, paratransit, school routes with the grade levels, motorcoach charters with the trip lengths, or airport and campus shuttles. "Two school routes, 110 students a day, kindergarten through eighth grade" tells a transportation director exactly what you have handled.

**Equipment.** Conventional and transit-style school buses, 35- and 40-foot transit buses, articulated buses, motorcoaches, cutaway shuttles. Name the transmission and fuel if the posting cares (diesel, CNG, electric), and name the wheelchair lift or ramp systems you have operated and secured.

**Safety record, in checkable facts.** Years without a preventable accident, passenger-injury-free trips, clean state or roadside inspections, and pre-trip and post-trip inspection discipline. A driver who can write "[N] years, zero preventable accidents, zero passenger injuries" has written the line that gets the interview.

**Passenger management.** For school routes: student management, discipline reporting, special-needs transportation with securement and individualized plans, and communication with parents and the school. For transit and paratransit: fare handling, Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) securement and announcements, de-escalation, and incident reporting. For charters: itineraries, luggage, and passenger communication over multi-day trips.

**Compliance you drove under.** The drug and alcohol testing program (Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration rules for CDL drivers, Federal Transit Administration rules at transit agencies), hours-of-service or agency scheduling rules, and any electronic logging or fleet telematics system the employer ran.

## Credentials and checks, as held

List every credential with its true status and date: the CDL class and endorsements, the DOT medical certificate, the state school bus certificate or training program, CPR and first aid, and passenger-assistance or defensive-driving training with the program name. Background check and fingerprint clearance are conditions of the job rather than resume lines, but if a posting asks for them, one line stating they are current is fine.

Do not list a school bus or passenger endorsement you have not yet earned as if you hold it. If you are enrolled in training, write it as in progress with the expected date.

## Moving between bus jobs

School to transit, transit to motorcoach, and shuttle to paratransit are common moves, and the resume should show the overlap and name the gap honestly. A school bus driver moving to transit already has the P endorsement, the medical card, and the safety record; what changes is fare handling, ADA securement on a lift-equipped transit bus, and a different testing program. Write the parts you have done, and let the interview cover the training the new employer provides. Inflating a shuttle job into transit experience, or a P endorsement into an S, is the kind of claim a licensing check catches immediately.

## Format notes

**Length follows your history, not a page rule.** A driver with ten years across a school district, a transit agency, and a charter company has more that matters than one page holds. **Two pages is normal and expected once you have more than one substantive job to describe.** Keep the routes, passengers, equipment, safety numbers, and credentials; cut the duties every driver shares.

Reverse chronological, single column, the license line above the work history, and no graphics or photos. School districts and transit agencies often run applications through screening software, and a two-column template can scramble the read. One exception worth knowing: **federal applications through USAJOBS cap at two pages** as of the September 2025 OPM change.

Gaps get one plain dated line. An employer verifying your driving employment history will see the gap, so the resume should say what the history says.

## A bullet bank you can adapt — keep only what's true

- "CDL Class [B/A], [state], endorsements P and S; DOT medical certificate current through [month/year]"
- "Drove [N] daily routes serving [N] passengers a day on [route type]; [N] years without a preventable accident"
- "Operated [35-foot/40-foot/articulated/motorcoach] buses; wheelchair lift and securement on every ADA trip"
- "Passed [N] state school bus inspections with no defects; completed pre-trip and post-trip inspections every shift"
- "Transported [N] students with individualized transportation plans; documented incidents per district policy"
- "Handled fares and passenger counts on fixed-route service; [N] complaint-free months"
- "Completed [state] school bus driver certification [year]; CPR and first aid current through [month/year]"
- "Trained [N] new drivers on route procedures and student management"

## What screening software looks for on a bus driver resume

Filters commonly read for: CDL Class B, Class A, passenger endorsement, school bus endorsement, DOT medical card, air brakes, pre-trip inspection, student management, special needs transportation, ADA, wheelchair lift, paratransit, fixed route, fare collection, defensive driving, CPR, first aid, clean MVR, and the dispatch or telematics systems the employer runs. [O*NET's profile for transit and intercity bus drivers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-3052.00) has the full task list, and school bus drivers have [their own profile](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/53-3051.00).

The highest-risk lines are the ones a licensing check verifies in minutes: an endorsement you do not hold, a lapsed medical card, a certificate you have not finished, or a safety record that disagrees with your motor vehicle record. Write what the license and the record show.
