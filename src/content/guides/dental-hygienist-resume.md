Dental offices read a hygienist resume in a set order: the license first, then the patients you see and the care you give, then the permits your state allows and the software you use. A resume that opens with "responsible for cleanings and patient education" leaves the license hunt to the reader and says nothing about how much care you deliver in a day.

The fix is a short licenses section at the top, then one line per job with the practice type and your schedule, then the procedures and numbers an office manager or dentist looks for.

## Lead with your license and your schedule

**Before:** "Dental hygienist responsible for cleanings, X-rays, and patient education."

**After:** "Registered Dental Hygienist (RDH), Ohio. General practice, 3 dentists: see 8 to 9 patients a day, including periodontal maintenance and scaling and root planing; take and review full-mouth and bitewing radiographs; and kept a 92% reappointment rate across 2025."

See 8 to 9 patients a day, give local anesthesia under a [State] permit, and chart in Dentrix." Name your license, your years, your setting, and one or two things an office cannot teach quickly.

## Your license, written exactly

Every state requires dental hygienists to be licensed, and the rules vary by state. In most states, licensure means graduating from an accredited dental hygiene program and passing written and clinical exams. The written exam is usually the National Board Dental Hygiene Examination, which the Joint Commission on National Dental Examinations says is [intended to fulfill or partially fulfill the written examination requirement](https://jcnde.ada.org/nbdhe) for licensure in all states.

On the resume:

- **Write the credential and the state:** "Registered Dental Hygienist (RDH), Ohio." Some states use a different title, such as Licensed Dental Hygienist, so use the one on your license.
- **List each state** if you hold more than one license.
- **Show each license's status honestly,** and keep up the continuing education your state requires to renew it. If a license has lapsed, leave it off or write its status the way your state board shows it, such as "expired 2024" or "inactive."
- **The license number is optional.** Offices verify licenses through the state dental board.
- **Write the clinical exam by its full name** and the testing agency that gave it, as your state dental board lists it, rather than "clinical exam" alone.

## Permits and certifications your state allows

What a hygienist may do beyond cleanings depends on the state, and the permits you hold are worth a line each:

- **Local anesthesia.** The American Dental Hygienists' Association's [state-by-state local anesthesia chart](https://www.adha.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ADHA-Local-Anesthesia-Chart_Dec2025.pdf), revised December 2025, lists every state and the District of Columbia as allowing hygienists to give local anesthesia, but the rules differ: the course hours, whether an exam is required, the level of dentist supervision, and in some states the types of injections. Texas, for example, began allowing it in 2023 and allows infiltration only. A few states authorized it only recently, such as Delaware in 2025, where the board's rules were still pending in December 2025, so check your state dental board for the current rule.
- **Nitrous oxide,** if your state allows hygienists to give or monitor it and you hold the permit.
- **Radiography,** if your state issues a separate certificate.
- **Restorative or other expanded functions,** in states that allow them after added training, written exactly as your state names the permit.
- **CPR.** Many jobs require current CPR certification, often a healthcare provider course such as BLS. Our guide on [how to list BLS and CPR](/guides/bls-and-cpr-on-resume/) shows the exact format.

Write each one by the name your state board uses, with the state: "Local Anesthesia Permit, [State] Board of Dentistry."

## The care you give, with numbers

- **Patients per day** on a typical schedule, and whether you work out of one or two operatories.
- **Procedures you perform most:** prophylaxis, periodontal maintenance, scaling and root planing, sealants, fluoride, and anything specialized, such as laser-assisted care where your state allows it.
- **Periodontal work:** full-mouth periodontal charting, and the share of your schedule that is periodontal patients, if you know it.
- **Radiographs:** the types you take, such as bitewings, full-mouth series, and panoramic images, and whether you use digital sensors.
- **Patient education:** home care plans, tobacco cessation talks, or nutrition counseling, described with what you actually do.
- **Office numbers,** only if your office tracks them and shares them with you: reappointment or recall rate, hygiene production per day, or treatment your patients accept.

Never put a patient's name, photo, or case details on a resume.

## Software and equipment

Name the practice management software you chart in, such as Dentrix, Eaglesoft, or Open Dental, because many postings name one. Add the equipment you use daily: ultrasonic scalers, intraoral cameras, digital radiography sensors, and lasers if your state and training allow them.

## The practice you work in

Write the setting next to each employer, because the work differs:

- **General practice,** with a mix of recall patients, children, and adults.
- **Periodontal practice,** with heavier periodontal therapy and maintenance.
- **Pediatric practice,** with sealants, fluoride, and young patients.
- **Group practices and dental service organizations,** often with production goals and several offices.
- **Public health, school sealant programs, community clinics, and long-term care,** with screenings, outreach, and patients who are harder to reach.

## New graduates and career changers

**New graduates** should lead with the license status, then the program and its clinical work: "Dental Hygiene, A.A.S., [College], 05/2026, a program accredited by the Commission on Dental Accreditation. Clinic: [number] patients, including [number] periodontal cases." Add rotations, community screenings, and research projects.

**Dental assistants who became hygienists** should keep the assisting years, because they show chairside experience, radiographs, and office systems. Our [dental assistant resume guide](/guides/dental-assistant-resume/) covers how to write that work.

**Temporary and per diem hygienists** can group the offices they covered under one entry: "Per Diem Dental Hygienist, 14 offices in the Columbus area, 2024 to present." If you worked through a staffing service, list the service as the employer.

## Format notes

- **Put a licenses and certifications section near the top,** right under your name or summary.
- **Use the title on your license** and the practice type for each job.
- **Keep it plain,** with standard headings and no tables, so screening software reads every line.
- **Leave out photos.** Many US employers prefer resumes without them.
- **Add professional memberships,** such as the American Dental Hygienists' Association, and continuing education that matches the posting, such as a laser or periodontal course.
- **One page is usually enough** for a new graduate or a few years in one or two offices, in our view; a second page is reasonable once you have several practices, specialties, or a public health role to describe.

## Dental hygienist resume bullets you can adapt — keep only what's true

- "See [number] patients a day in a [practice type] with [number] dentists."
- "Perform periodontal maintenance and scaling and root planing for [number] patients a week."
- "Take and review [radiograph types] with digital sensors."
- "Kept a [X]% reappointment rate over [period]."
- "Give local anesthesia under a [State] permit for [procedures]."
- "Chart in [Dentrix / Eaglesoft / Open Dental]; build home care plans for every periodontal patient."
