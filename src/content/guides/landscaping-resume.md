A landscaping resume is read by a crew leader, a branch or account manager, or the owner of a landscaping company. They are looking for proof that you can do the work without being watched: which equipment you run safely, what kinds of properties you have maintained or built, what licenses you hold, and whether you show up through a hot summer and a busy spring. A resume that says "experienced landscaper, hard worker, good with plants" tells them none of that.

Landscaping work is counted by the property and the day. Routes, crews, and job tickets usually hold the numbers for a strong resume. Put them on the page.

## Lead with your properties, your equipment, and your credentials

**Before:** "Landscaper responsible for mowing, trimming, and general yard maintenance."

**After:** "Landscape crew member for a [commercial maintenance company]: mowed and maintained about [12] commercial properties a day on a [3]-person crew, ran [zero-turn and stand-on mowers], string trimmers, and blowers, installed [N] cubic yards of mulch a season; [state] commercial pesticide applicator license ([category]); returned for [4] seasons."

## Licenses and certifications, named exactly

**Pesticide applicator license.** Federal rules require [certification for commercial applicators](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/171.101) who use or supervise the use of restricted use pesticides, with categories such as ornamental and turf pest control. States run the testing, and the [competency standards](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/171.103) require a written exam in the core standards and in each category. Many states also license commercial application of other pesticides, and a few, such as Florida, Maryland, and Virginia, also certify commercial fertilizer applicators. If you hold a license, write the state, the category, and the expiration date. If you applied products under someone else's license, describe it that way. The EPA's [applicator certification page](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-worker-safety/certification-standards-pesticide-applicators) explains how the federal and state rules fit together.

**Arborist certification.** The [ISA Certified Arborist](https://www.isa-arbor.com/Credentials/Types-of-Credentials/ISA-Certified-Arborist) credential requires three years of full-time arboriculture experience or a combination of education and practical experience, plus an exam. If you hold it, put it near the top. If you do tree work without it, describe the work: pruning, removals, climbing, rigging, and chipper and stump grinder operation.

**Industry certifications.** The National Association of Landscape Professionals offers [Landscape Industry Certified](https://www.landscapeprofessionals.org/LP/Certification) credentials for technicians and managers, and the Irrigation Association offers [irrigation certifications](https://www.irrigation.org/ia/Certification/Landscape-Certifications.aspx) such as irrigation technician and auditor. Some states also license irrigation contractors. Write each credential with its issuer and year.

**Safety and driving.** Chainsaw safety training, first aid and CPR, and heat illness training belong on the resume with their dates. A clean driving record and experience towing an equipment trailer are worth one line, because crews move between properties all day.

## Equipment and skills

**Equipment.** Name what you run: zero-turn, stand-on, and walk-behind mowers, string trimmers, edgers, blowers, hedge trimmers, aerators, dethatchers, sod cutters, skid steers, mini excavators, plate compactors, chainsaws, chippers, and stump grinders. Say what you can run on your own.

**Maintenance.** Describe mowing, edging, pruning shrubs, bed maintenance, mulching, leaf cleanup, aeration and seeding, and turf care. If you learned to diagnose turf or plant problems, say so.

**Installation and hardscape.** Describe planting trees and shrubs, laying sod, grading, installing pavers, retaining walls, and drainage, and the size of the jobs, such as square feet of pavers or linear feet of wall.

**Irrigation.** Describe installing, repairing, and adjusting irrigation systems, setting controllers, and finding leaks. Water savings from a system audit or a controller change are a strong number.

**Snow and ice.** If your company did snow removal, name the equipment you ran, the properties you cleared, and the hours you worked through storms.

## The numbers a landscaping manager reads

Give properties maintained per day or per week, the type of properties (residential, commercial, municipal, HOA, or campus), crew size, the jobs you installed and their size, and how many seasons you returned. If you led a crew, write how many people, how many properties, and how you kept the route on schedule.

## Moving up

Lines that point upward matter: running the route when the crew leader is out, training new crew members, earning a license, or moving from maintenance into installation, irrigation, or tree work. If you are aiming at a crew leader or account manager role, add the crew size, the route, and the customers you handled. ## Format notes

**Length follows your history.** A landscaper with one or two employers usually fits on one page. **Two pages is normal and expected once you have more than one substantive job to describe,** such as several companies, a business of your own, or a mix of landscaping and construction. Keep the equipment, the licenses, the properties, and the numbers; cut the duties every crew shares.

Use reverse chronological order and a single column, and leave out graphics and photos. Many larger landscaping companies run applications through hiring software, and a two-column template can scramble the read. Put licenses and certifications in their own section near the top.

Seasonal gaps are normal. Write each season with its dates and give any other gap one plain dated line.

## Landscaping resume examples: a bullet bank you can adapt — keep only what's true

- "Mowed and maintained about [N] [commercial/residential] properties a day on a [N]-person crew"
- "Ran [zero-turn/stand-on] mowers, trimmers, edgers, and blowers; trained new crew members on safe operation"
- "Installed [N] square feet of pavers and [N] linear feet of retaining wall in [year]"
- "Installed and repaired irrigation systems on [N] properties; cut water use by [N]% after controller adjustments"
- "[State] commercial pesticide applicator license, [category], expires [month/year]"
- "ISA Certified Arborist ([year]); pruned and removed trees with climbing and rigging"
- "Ran the route as acting crew leader on [N] days; finished every property on schedule"
- "Returned for [N] seasons; ran snow removal on [N] properties through the winter"

## What screening software looks for on a landscaping resume

Filters commonly read for: landscaper, landscape technician, groundskeeper, lawn care, turf, mowing, zero-turn mower, trimming, edging, pruning, mulching, planting, sod, hardscape, pavers, retaining wall, irrigation, sprinkler, pesticide applicator license, herbicide, fertilizer, arborist, tree care, chainsaw, skid steer, snow removal, equipment maintenance, and customer service. [O*NET's profile for landscaping and groundskeeping workers](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/37-3011.00) has the full task list.

The highest-risk lines are the ones an owner can check with one call: a license in someone else's name, equipment you have never run alone, or an installation you only watched. Write what you actually did.
