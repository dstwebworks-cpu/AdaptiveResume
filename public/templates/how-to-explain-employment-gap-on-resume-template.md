# Gap-Explanation Phrasing Sheet
### A free one-pager from AdaptiveResume

One accurate line beats a nervous paragraph. Adapt the phrasing that matches your truth — and only that one.

## Does your gap even need a line?
- Under ~6 months, or years in the past → usually no. Normal job searches take months; readers know it.
- ~6+ months, recent, or mid-career → yes. If a stranger would pause at your dates and wonder, answer before they ask.

## The one-liners (use on the resume, in your work-history timeline)
| Situation | Phrasing to adapt |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Caregiving | "Full-time caregiver for a family member, [MM/YYYY] – [MM/YYYY]" |
| Health | "Personal medical leave, resolved — [year]" (no diagnosis owed; "resolved" answers the real question) |
| Layoff | "Position eliminated in company-wide reduction, [MM/YYYY]" |
| Education / retraining | "Completed [program/credential], [year] – [year]" (list under Education — often stops reading as a gap at all) |
| Job search | Optional line; if asked: "Searching deliberately; turned down roles that weren't a fit" — only if true |

If real skills stayed warm during the gap (bookkeeping, coordination, volunteering), one factual second line is fair game.

## Where each explanation belongs
- **Resume:** the one factual line. States, doesn't discuss.
- **Cover letter:** 2–3 sentences if recent and significant — what it was, that it's concluded, why you're ready now.
- **Interview:** the calm full version, rehearsed once out loud, ending forward: "...and here's what I'm bringing to this role."

## The date-format trade-off, honestly
- "2021 – 2024" (years only): a real convention; hides small gaps — and recruiters know that's often why it's chosen. Application forms may demand months anyway.
- "03/2021 – 06/2024" (months): confident, nothing to discover. Our recommendation.
- Either is honest ONLY if the dates are accurate. Formatting is a choice; changed dates are a lie.

## Never — both of these get checked
1. No invented employers, consulting shells, or "family business" roles that didn't happen — employment verification exists.
2. No stretched dates — they contradict the previous employer's records.

An explained gap is a detail. A discovered cover-up ends the process.
